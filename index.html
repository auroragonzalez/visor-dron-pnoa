What you are looking at

A cell-by-cell comparison of two LiDAR sources over Santomera (Murcia, Spain), aggregated to a common 10 m grid:

  • Drone LiDAR (2026) — 350–470 pts/m², the reference ("ground truth" of forest structure).
  • PNOA LiDAR (2023) — Spain's national airborne coverage, ~5 pts/m², ~100× sparser.

The question: how wrong is the low-density national LiDAR for wildfire-fuel metrics, and can a learned calibration bring it up to drone level?

Layers

  • Drone (ground truth): the fuel metric computed from the drone point cloud.
  • PNOA raw: the same metric definitions computed from the sparse PNOA points, using PNOA's own ground surface — what you would get anywhere in Spain without a drone.
  • PNOA calibrated: the raw PNOA metric passed through a gradient-boosting model trained to reproduce the drone values. Shown with spatially out-of-fold predictions, so no cell is scored by a model that saw it in training.
  • Residual (raw / calibrated): prediction − drone. Red = overestimation, blue = underestimation, near-white = good agreement. Switching between the two residuals shows what the calibration actually fixes.
  • Outside 90% conformal interval: each calibrated cell carries a prediction interval (±q). Cells in red are where the drone value falls outside that interval — about 10% of cells are expected to fail by construction; watch whether failures cluster spatially.

Metrics (buttons)

  • Canopy height p95: 95th percentile of vegetation return heights (m).
  • Canopy cover: fraction of returns above 2 m.
  • Understorey / Mid-storey: fraction of vegetation returns in the 1–5 m and 5–15 m strata — the layers that carry fire into the canopy.

Change 2023–26 (orange overlay)

Cells where the structure really changed between the two flights: pine mortality/felling over ~12.4 ha, confirmed with 2022–2026 orthophotos. For these cells the PNOA–drone disagreement is real change, not measurement error, so they are excluded from calibration.

Why do orange cells still show green underneath? The overlay only tints the current metric value; it does not erase it. Inside the affected patch, (a) dead trees often remain standing — snags still return LiDAR points and still measure as canopy height — and (b) at 10 m resolution a cell is only partially affected. Height only drops when trees actually fall or are felled, so it is normal to see canopy values surviving inside orange cells.

Panels and conventions

  • Top-right: MAE before/after calibration and the empirical coverage of the 90% interval.
  • All three sources share the same colour scale per metric, so switching layers is a fair comparison.
  • Click any cell for its exact values and whether the interval covers it.
  • PNOA is ETRS89/UTM30 and the drone WGS84/UTM30N; the offset is <1 m, negligible at 10 m.
